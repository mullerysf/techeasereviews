Your First Day in Florida:
The Snowbird Tech Setup Guide

By Shawn · Simply Connected · Venice, FL · Updated October 2026

You just drove 1,200 miles, the condo smells like it's been closed since April, and you can't get your Wi-Fi to work. Here's what to tackle first — in the right order — so everything is running by tonight.

📍 For: Florida snowbirds returning for the season
⏱ Time: 60–90 minutes to get fully set up
🛠 Covers: Wi-Fi · Phone · Streaming · Security

Every October and November, I get the same calls from snowbirds who just arrived in Venice, Sarasota, and the surrounding areas. The Wi-Fi password is wrong. Netflix wants them to verify their location. The phone is still on the northern carrier. The TV remote doesn't work. The laptop wants 47 updates before it'll do anything.

None of this is complicated — but it hits you all at once when you're already tired from the drive. After helping hundreds of snowbirds get settled, I put together this guide in the order that actually works. Start at the top and work your way down.

📡

Step 1: Get the Wi-Fi Working First

Do this before anything else. Every other step is easier — or only possible — once you have a reliable internet connection.

1
Locate your router and power it on

Find the router — usually in a closet, entertainment center, or utility room. Make sure it's plugged in and the lights are on. Wait a full 2 minutes before testing anything. Routers need time to connect to the internet after powering up.

2
Find your Wi-Fi name and password

Check the sticker on the bottom or back of your router. Your network name (SSID) and password are usually printed right there. If you changed them and can't remember, do a factory reset: hold the reset button for 10 seconds, then use the default password on the sticker.

3
Connect your phone first, test the speed

Go to Settings → Wi-Fi on your phone and connect. Then open a browser and go to fast.com — it should show at least 25 Mbps for normal use. If it shows under 10 Mbps or won't load at all, call your internet provider before wasting time setting up other devices.

4
Connect your other devices one at a time

TV, tablet, laptop — connect them to Wi-Fi now so they can start downloading updates in the background while you continue setup. Don't wait for updates to finish before moving on.

💡 If the Wi-Fi signal is weak in your bedroom
A router in one corner of a condo often struggles to reach the far bedroom. A simple fix: a Wi-Fi extender ($30–40 at any hardware store) plugged halfway between the router and the weak area. Or consider a mesh router system — I cover the best options in my Wi-Fi routers for seniors guide.

Step 2: Set Up Your Smartphone for Florida

Your phone works differently in Florida than it did up north — and a few quick adjustments save you from slow data, roaming charges, and missed calls.

1
Turn on Wi-Fi Calling

If your Florida condo gets weak cell signal, Wi-Fi Calling lets your phone make and receive calls over your home internet instead of the cell tower. On iPhone: Settings → Phone → Wi-Fi Calling → turn it on. On Android: Settings → Connections → Wi-Fi Calling. This is a game-changer in buildings with thick concrete walls.

2
Check that your plan covers Florida

Most major carriers (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile) include Florida in their standard plans. But if you're on a regional carrier, check your coverage map — you may be roaming. Call your carrier if your data feels unusually slow.

3
Update your apps and operating system

Go to your phone's Settings and check for a software update. Then open the App Store (iPhone) or Play Store (Android) and update all your apps. Do this while connected to Wi-Fi — it can use several gigabytes of data.

4
Make text larger if needed

On iPhone: Settings → Display & Brightness → Text Size, then drag the slider right. On Android: Settings → Accessibility → Font Size. Bigger text reduces eye strain and makes everything easier to use. There's no wrong answer — make it as big as you need.

Step 3: Get Your Streaming Services Working

This is where most snowbirds run into trouble. Streaming services like Netflix and Disney+ now use location verification — meaning they want to confirm you're actually in the United States when you're here for the season. Here's how to handle each one.

1
Netflix — set your location

Netflix now enforces household location. Open Netflix on your TV and go to Account Settings on the Netflix website. Look for "Manage Your Household" and follow the steps to add your Florida location. You'll get a verification email or text. Complete it within 15 minutes.

2
Amazon Prime Video — just sign in

Prime Video is the easiest — just sign into your Amazon account on the TV or streaming device. No location verification needed. If you're already signed in but it's not working, sign out and sign back in.

3
Hulu and Disney+ — password reset if needed

If you haven't logged into Hulu or Disney+ since last season, your session may have expired. Go to the app's sign-in screen and use "Forgot Password" to reset — it emails you a link. Use your email on your phone to click the link, then set a new password and use it on the TV.

4
Cable or antenna — check your local channels

If you use a cable box, turn it on and let it download its program guide — this can take 10–15 minutes. If you use an antenna for local channels, do a channel scan: on your TV remote, go to Settings → Channels → Scan for Channels. You'll pick up the local Venice/Sarasota stations.

⚠️ Write your passwords down before you lose them
This is the #1 call I get from snowbirds. Your Netflix, Hulu, Amazon, and email passwords were probably saved on your home computer up north — but they're not saved on your Florida devices. Before you leave home next spring, write them on a piece of paper and keep it in your wallet or a secure place at the condo. A simple password notebook works great.
🔐

Step 4: Get Your Email and Important Accounts Working

Your bank, email, and health accounts may send security alerts when you sign in from a new location. That's normal and a good sign — it means they're protecting you. Here's how to handle it smoothly.

1
Sign into your email first

Open your email app — Gmail, Outlook, or whatever you use — and make sure it's working. Many verification codes for other accounts come to your email, so this needs to work before you try anything else.

2
Check your bank and financial accounts

Log into your bank's app or website. If they ask for a verification code, check your email or phone for a text message. Enter the code when prompted. This is normal and safe — it's just confirming it's really you signing in from a new location.

3
Verify your Medicare and health insurance apps

Open your Medicare app, any supplemental insurance apps, and your pharmacy app (like CVS or Walgreens). Make sure they're signed in. If you need prescriptions filled at a Florida pharmacy, your app will show your coverage — much easier than carrying all your insurance cards.

🛡️

Step 5: Know the Scams That Target Snowbirds

Florida has some of the highest rates of senior scams in the country — and the first few weeks of snowbird season are when scammers are most active, because they know people are getting reconnected and may be confused about their accounts.

🚨 Four scams to watch for right now
1. The "verify your location" scam. You get a call or text saying your Netflix or Amazon account has been flagged and you need to call a number to verify. Ignore it — go directly to the app or website instead.

2. The tech support pop-up. A website shows a scary warning saying your computer has a virus and to call a toll-free number. Close the browser tab. Do not call the number.

3. The utility company threat. Someone calls saying your FPL (Florida Power & Light) will be shut off unless you pay immediately with a gift card. Real utilities never ask for gift cards.

4. The contractor at the door. Someone comes to your door saying they noticed damage to your roof or driveway and can fix it cheap today. Get at least three quotes from licensed contractors first.

Bonus: If You Brought a Tablet or Laptop

Tablets and laptops need a few extra minutes of attention when you arrive for the season.

1
Connect to Wi-Fi and let updates run

Connect your tablet or laptop to your Florida Wi-Fi first thing and leave it plugged in while you do other setup tasks. Updates can take 30–60 minutes on a device that's been off since last spring.

2
Sign back into apps that signed you out

After months of inactivity, apps like your email, banking apps, and streaming services may have logged you out for security. Plan 15 minutes to re-enter your credentials on each one.

3
Check your storage space

On iPad: Settings → General → iPad Storage. On Android tablet: Settings → About Device → Storage. If you're below 10% free space, delete photos you've already backed up or apps you don't use. A full device runs slow and can't receive updates.

Common Questions from Arriving Snowbirds

My Wi-Fi was working fine last spring. Why isn't it connecting now?

The most common reason is that your router needed to reconnect to the internet after sitting unused. Give it 2–3 full minutes after powering on. If it still won't connect, try unplugging it for 30 seconds and plugging it back in. If the internet light on your router stays red or off after 5 minutes, call your internet provider — your service may have been disconnected for non-use or your account may need to be reactivated.

Netflix says my account is being used in a different location. What do I do?

This is Netflix's household verification feature — it confirms that you're actually living where you say you are. Sign into Netflix on a web browser (not the app) and look for "Manage Your Household" in your account settings. Follow the prompts to add your Florida location. You'll receive a verification link to your email address. Click it within 15 minutes and you should be good to go.

My phone signal is really weak inside my condo. Can I fix this?

Yes — this is very common in concrete buildings. The fix is Wi-Fi Calling: your phone routes calls and texts over your home internet instead of the cell tower, so you get a perfect connection inside even when the cell signal is weak. On iPhone: Settings → Phone → Wi-Fi Calling → switch it on. On Android: Settings → Connections → Wi-Fi Calling. Your carrier may need to have Wi-Fi Calling enabled on your account — call them if the option doesn't appear.

I forgot the password for my email / Netflix / bank. What now?

Use the "Forgot Password" link on the sign-in page. The reset instructions will be sent to your email address — which is why it's important to get email working first. If you've also forgotten your email password, you'll need to reset that first using your phone number for verification. Going forward, keep a written record of your passwords somewhere safe — a dedicated password notebook kept in a drawer at your condo works well.

How long does it take to get everything set up when I arrive?

With this guide, plan on 60–90 minutes if everything goes smoothly. The bulk of that is waiting for updates to download. Wi-Fi setup takes about 10 minutes, phone setup another 10, streaming services 15–20 minutes (Netflix verification is the most time-consuming), and email/accounts another 10–15 minutes. If you run into a snag — forgotten password, router not connecting, streaming service giving errors — each of those can add 20–30 minutes.

Is it safe to do online banking from my Florida condo's Wi-Fi?

Yes — your home Wi-Fi network is private and secure. What you want to avoid is doing banking on public Wi-Fi (a coffee shop, the condo pool area's free Wi-Fi, a library). On your own home network with your own password, banking is safe. Using your bank's app rather than a browser adds an extra layer of security as well.

Do I need a separate phone plan for Florida, or does mine work everywhere?

The three major carriers — Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile — all include nationwide coverage in their standard plans, so Florida is covered at no extra charge. If you're on a smaller regional carrier, check your plan details or call them to confirm. The Venice/Sarasota area has excellent coverage from all three major carriers.

I got an email saying my Amazon/Netflix account was hacked right after I arrived. Is this real?

Be very cautious. Scammers time these fake security emails to coincide with snowbird season, knowing people are logging into accounts from new locations. Before clicking anything in the email, go directly to the service's website by typing it into your browser (amazon.com, netflix.com). Check your account there. If there's a real problem, you'll see it — if everything looks fine, the email was a scam. Never call a phone number from a security email.
