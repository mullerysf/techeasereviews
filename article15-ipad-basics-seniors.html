The physical buttons and what they do · How to turn on and unlock your iPad · How to open and close apps · How to make a FaceTime video call · How to browse the internet · How to adjust text size · How to connect to Wi-Fi · The most common problems and how to fix them

I set up iPads for seniors throughout the Venice, Florida area every week. Most of my clients have never used a tablet before — or used one briefly and gave up because nobody explained it clearly. This guide covers everything a complete beginner needs to start using an iPad confidently, written the way I explain it in person: plainly, patiently, and without assuming you already know anything.

Work through this at your own pace. There's no rush. Read one section, try it on your iPad, then come back for the next one.

Lesson 1: The Buttons — What Each One Does

Your iPad has only a few physical buttons. Understanding what each one does eliminates most early confusion.

⬆️
Top Button
Press once to wake the screen. Press and hold for 2 seconds to turn the iPad off (a slider appears — slide it). On most iPads this button also has Touch ID — your fingerprint unlocks the iPad when you press it.
🔊
Volume Buttons (Side)
Two buttons on the right side. The top one makes sound louder, the bottom one makes it quieter. Use these for FaceTime calls, videos, and music. Press repeatedly to adjust — you'll see a volume bar on screen.
🏠
Home Button (older iPads)
The round button at the bottom of older iPad models. Press it once to go back to your home screen from anywhere. Also has Touch ID fingerprint sensor. Newer iPads don't have this button — they use swipe gestures instead.
🔌
Charging Port (Bottom)
The slot at the bottom where you plug in your charging cable. Newer iPads use USB-C (oval shaped). Older iPads use Lightning (small rectangular). The cable only goes in one way — if it doesn't slide in easily, flip it over.
💡 Nothing to Fear

You cannot break your iPad by pressing the wrong button or tapping the wrong thing. The worst that can happen is something unexpected appears on screen — and pressing the top button or Home button always gets you back to a familiar place.

Lesson 2: Turning On, Unlocking, and Going Home

2

Basic Navigation

The three things you'll do dozens of times per day

1
Turning the screen on
Press the top button once. The screen lights up showing the time and date. If nothing happens, the battery may be dead — plug it in and wait a few minutes.
2
Unlocking with Touch ID
After the screen wakes up, press the top button (or Home button on older iPads) with your registered finger. The iPad unlocks immediately. If it doesn't recognize your fingerprint, try again or enter your passcode.
3
Getting to the home screen
On older iPads with a Home button: press the Home button once from anywhere. On newer iPads without a Home button: swipe up from the very bottom edge of the screen. You'll see all your app icons — this is your starting point.
4
Turning the screen off
Press the top button once. The screen goes dark. The iPad is still on — it's just sleeping to save battery. Press the top button again to wake it up. Your iPad does not need to be "turned off" — just let the screen sleep.

Lesson 3: Opening and Closing Apps

3

Working With Apps

How to open, use, and close the apps on your iPad

1
Opening an app
Tap the app icon once with your finger. The app opens. If the app doesn't open immediately, tap it again — sometimes it takes a second on older devices.
2
Going back to the home screen
Press the Home button (older iPads) or swipe up from the bottom (newer iPads). The app closes and you're back at your home screen. You don't need to "save" anything — the iPad remembers where you were.
3
Switching between apps
On newer iPads: swipe up from the bottom and pause in the middle — you'll see all your recently used apps as cards. Tap any card to switch to that app. On older iPads: double-press the Home button to see recent apps.
4
Scrolling up and down
Put your finger on the screen and slide it up — the page scrolls down to show more content. Slide down to scroll back up. This works in websites, emails, and most apps. Use one finger and a gentle sliding motion.

Lesson 4: Connecting to Wi-Fi

4

Wi-Fi Connection

How to connect your iPad to your home internet

1
Open Settings
Tap the gray gear icon on your home screen. This opens the Settings app — the control center for your iPad.
Home screen → Settings (gray gear icon)
2
Tap Wi-Fi
In the Settings menu, tap "Wi-Fi" near the top of the list. You'll see a list of available Wi-Fi networks nearby.
Settings → Wi-Fi
3
Find your network name
Look for your home Wi-Fi network name in the list. It's usually printed on a sticker on your router. Tap your network name to select it.
4
Enter your password
A keyboard appears. Type your Wi-Fi password — it's also usually on the router sticker. Passwords are case-sensitive (capital letters matter). Tap "Join" when done.
5
Confirm you're connected
A checkmark appears next to your network name and a Wi-Fi symbol appears at the top of the screen. You're connected. Your iPad will remember this network automatically in the future.

Lesson 5: Making a FaceTime Video Call

5

FaceTime Video Calls

The most popular thing seniors use their iPad for

1
Open FaceTime
Tap the green FaceTime icon on your home screen. It looks like a video camera on a green background.
2
Tap the + button to start a new call
In the top right corner of FaceTime, tap the + symbol. A box appears where you can type a name or phone number.
3
Type the name or number
Start typing the name of the person you want to call. If they're in your contacts, their name will appear as a suggestion — tap it to select them.
4
Tap the green Video button
Tap the green camera icon to start a video call. The call rings on their device. You'll see yourself in a small box and them in the large area when they answer.
5
Ending the call
Tap the red circle button at the bottom of the screen. The call ends. The red button only appears when you tap the screen — if you don't see it, tap anywhere on the screen once.
💡 Answering a FaceTime Call

When someone calls you on FaceTime, your iPad makes a ringing sound and shows their name and photo. Tap the green button to answer. Tap the red button to decline. If you miss a call, their name appears in your FaceTime app — tap it to call them back.

Lesson 6: Making Text Larger

6

Larger Text & Display Zoom

The two most important settings for comfortable senior use

1
Open Settings
Tap the gray gear icon on your home screen.
2
Make text larger
Tap Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Larger Text → drag the slider to the right to increase text size. Watch the sample text above the slider change as you drag. Stop when the size feels comfortable.
Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Larger Text
3
Enable Display Zoom
Go back to Settings → Display & Brightness → Display Zoom → select "Larger Text" → tap Set → tap Use Zoomed. Everything on screen becomes larger — icons, buttons, and text.
Settings → Display & Brightness → Display Zoom → Larger Text

Common Problems and How to Fix Them

❓ My iPad screen is black and won't turn on
Press the top button once. If nothing happens, the battery is likely dead — plug it into the charger and wait 5-10 minutes, then try again. Make sure the charging cable is fully plugged in at both ends.
❓ I can't hear anything on FaceTime or videos
Press the volume up button on the right side of the iPad several times. Also check that the small switch above the volume buttons (if your iPad has one) is not switched to silent mode. If still no sound, go to Settings → Sounds and make sure the volume slider is up.
❓ My iPad is frozen and not responding
Press and hold the top button and either volume button simultaneously for about 10 seconds until the Apple logo appears. This restarts the iPad without losing anything. It's safe to do anytime.
❓ I accidentally deleted an app
Go to the App Store (blue icon with an "A") → tap your profile picture in the top right → tap Purchased → find the app you deleted → tap the download button next to it. The app reinstalls for free.
❓ My FaceTime isn't working
First check that Wi-Fi is connected (Wi-Fi symbol at the top of screen). Then go to Settings → FaceTime → make sure the green toggle is ON. If the other person can't receive your call, make sure they also have FaceTime turned on and are connected to Wi-Fi or cellular.
❓ I forgot my passcode
If Touch ID isn't working, you'll need the passcode. Try the numbers you commonly use. If truly forgotten, the iPad can be reset through a computer — but this erases everything. This is why writing down your Apple ID and passcode somewhere safe is so important when you first set it up.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from seniors who are new to iPad.

Q How do I use an iPad for the first time as a senior?
Start with just two things: turning it on and making a FaceTime call. Don't try to learn everything at once. On day one, practice turning the screen on and off and unlocking with Touch ID. On day two, try opening and closing one app. On day three, make a FaceTime call to a family member who expects it. Building confidence one skill at a time is far more effective than trying to learn the whole device in one session. Most seniors I work with are comfortably using their iPad for FaceTime, streaming, and browsing within 2-3 weeks of starting slowly like this.
Q What is the easiest way to learn iPad for seniors?
The easiest way to learn iPad as a senior is to have one specific goal — usually FaceTime with family — and practice just that one thing until it feels natural. Then add one skill at a time. Trying to learn everything at once leads to overwhelm. Having a family member or friend sit with you for the first hour makes an enormous difference — not to do things for you, but to answer questions as they come up in real time. Apple Stores also offer free "Today at Apple" sessions specifically for beginners, including seniors.
Q Is there an iPad guide for seniors for dummies?
This guide is written to serve that purpose — plain English, step by step, no assumptions. There is also a published book called "iPad for Seniors for Dummies" available on Amazon that covers similar ground in book format. For hands-on help in person, Simply Connected provides in-home iPad training for seniors throughout the Venice, Florida area — sometimes having someone sit with you and explain things face-to-face is the most effective way to learn.
Q How do I make my iPad easier to use as a senior?
Four settings make the biggest difference: (1) Display Zoom — makes everything larger (Settings → Display & Brightness → Display Zoom → Larger Text). (2) Larger Text — increases text size throughout all apps (Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Larger Text). (3) Touch ID — eliminates the need to type a passcode (Settings → Touch ID & Passcode). (4) Simplified home screen — remove all apps you don't use and keep only 6-8 on the first page. These four changes together make an iPad significantly easier to use for seniors.
Q What can seniors use an iPad for?
The most popular uses for seniors are: FaceTime video calls with family (the most common reason seniors get an iPad), streaming Netflix, Hulu, and Prime Video, reading books on Kindle and news on Safari, email to stay in touch, video calling doctors for telehealth appointments, music through Apple Music or Pandora, photo viewing of family photos, and games like solitaire and word puzzles. Most seniors end up using 3-4 of these regularly and find the iPad becomes an important part of daily life within a few weeks.
Q How do I get help with my iPad as a senior?
Several options: Family members — the most accessible help, especially for FaceTime setup. Apple Store — free "Today at Apple" classes and Genius Bar appointments for technical issues. Apple Support — call 1-800-275-2273 for phone support. In-home tech support — services like Simply Connected (Venice, FL area) come to your home and walk you through everything patiently. For seniors who find phone support difficult, in-home help is often the most effective option because everything is explained in your own environment on your own device.
