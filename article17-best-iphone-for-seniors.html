The iPhone 16 Plus has the same software and features as the standard iPhone 16 but with a significantly larger 6.7" display. Essential Settings to Change First

These are the first settings I change on every iPhone I set up for a senior client. They make a meaningful difference in daily comfort and usability.

🔤
Larger Text Size
Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Larger Text
Drag the slider right to increase text size throughout all apps. Set 2-3 steps above default for most seniors.
📵
Silence Unknown Callers
Settings → Phone → Silence Unknown Callers → ON
Automatically silences calls from numbers not in contacts. Eliminates most robocalls without any ongoing effort.
🔊
Increase Ringer Volume
Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Ringer and Alerts
Drag the slider all the way right. Also enable "Change with Buttons" so volume buttons adjust the ringer.
🅱️
Bold Text
Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Bold Text
Makes all text throughout the iPhone heavier and easier to read — especially helpful for seniors with mild vision changes.
📞
Wi-Fi Calling
Settings → Phone → Wi-Fi Calling → ON
Routes calls through home Wi-Fi when cell signal is weak. Eliminates dropped calls at home — one of the most impactful settings for seniors.
🏥
Medical ID
Health app → your profile → Medical ID
Stores emergency medical information accessible from the lock screen. Critical for seniors — fill in medications, conditions, and emergency contacts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about choosing the best iPhone for seniors — answered from real experience.

Q What is the best iPhone for seniors in 2026?
The iPhone 16 is the best iPhone for most seniors in 2026. It has a large 6.1" display, Face ID for effortless unlocking, FaceTime built in, and iOS 18 with improved accessibility features. At $799 it's a significant purchase but it will last 5-6 years with proper care and software support through approximately 2030. For seniors with vision challenges, the iPhone 16 Plus at $899 offers a larger 6.7" screen that makes everything easier to see and tap. Avoid the iPhone SE — its 4.7" screen is too small for comfortable senior use.
Q What is the easiest iPhone to use for seniors?
The iPhone 16 is the easiest iPhone for seniors when set up correctly. All current iPhones use the same iOS operating system, so "easiest" is more about setup than model. The settings that make the biggest difference: larger text size, Silence Unknown Callers, bold text, and Wi-Fi Calling. With those four settings configured, the iPhone 16 is genuinely simple for seniors to use for calls, FaceTime, and texting. Face ID also helps — seniors no longer need to remember or type a passcode to unlock the phone.
Q What is the best iPhone for elderly people?
For elderly people, the key iPhone features are screen size, ease of unlocking, and call quality. The iPhone 16 delivers on all three — 6.1" display, Face ID, and excellent call quality. For elderly people with significant vision challenges, the iPhone 16 Plus with its 6.7" screen is worth the extra cost. For elderly people with arthritis or dexterity challenges, Face ID is particularly valuable — they don't need to position a finger precisely on a sensor, just look at the phone. The iPhone SE's smaller screen makes it the worst choice for elderly users despite being the cheapest.
Q Is iPhone or Android better for seniors?
For most seniors, iPhone is the better choice — primarily because of the ecosystem. If the senior's family uses iPhones, FaceTime works seamlessly, iMessages are instant, and sharing photos is effortless. Apple also provides longer software support (5-6 years vs 2-3 for most Android phones) and the iOS interface is more consistent between updates. Android is a reasonable choice for seniors who strongly prefer it or whose family primarily uses Android — in which case a Samsung Galaxy is the best Android option for seniors. See our guide on best phones for seniors for a full iPhone vs Android comparison.
Q What iPhone size is best for seniors?
For seniors, bigger is almost always better. The iPhone 16 at 6.1" is the minimum I'd recommend for senior use. The iPhone 16 Plus at 6.7" is the better choice for anyone with vision challenges or large hands. Avoid the iPhone SE at 4.7" — the screen is too small for reading, texting, and FaceTime comfortably. The Pro Max at 6.9" is the largest option but at $1,199 it's expensive for features seniors don't need. The sweet spot for most seniors is the standard iPhone 16 at 6.1" or the Plus at 6.7".
Q How do I set up an iPhone for a senior parent?
Set it up before handing it over — don't give them a box and expect them to figure it out. Key steps: (1) Sign in with their Apple ID or create one. (2) Enable larger text — Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Larger Text. (3) Turn on Silence Unknown Callers — Settings → Phone → Silence Unknown Callers. (4) Set up FaceTime and do a test call. (5) Add family contacts with photos so they know who's calling. (6) Fill in Medical ID — Health app → Medical ID. (7) Enable Wi-Fi Calling. See our full iPhone setup guide for senior parents for a complete step-by-step walkthrough.
Q What is the best simple phone for seniors?
If simplicity is the top priority and smartphone features aren't needed, the Lively Flip2 (formerly GreatCall) is a good option — it's a basic flip phone designed specifically for seniors with large buttons and a simple interface. However for most seniors in 2026, the iPhone 16 set up correctly is actually simpler in daily use than a basic phone because Face ID eliminates the need to remember a passcode, and the large touch screen is more forgiving than small physical buttons. The key is proper setup — an iPhone configured for a senior is genuinely simple to use.
